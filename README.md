# Sawubona Community — TKZ Help Hub

### Built for Thokoza Communities — Ubuntu Spirit

> _"I am because we are."_

Sawubona Community is a mobile-first community help app designed for the residents of Thokoza township and surrounding areas (Eden Park, Thinasonke, Katlehong, Vosloorus). It brings essential services, civic reporting, mutual aid, and a trusted local services directory into one simple, warm, and accessible app that works perfectly on any smartphone.

---

## The Problem We're Solving

Township communities face real, daily challenges that existing tools don't address well:

1. **Hard-to-find emergency and municipal contacts** — When a pipe bursts at 11 PM or a street light goes out, residents struggle to find the right number to call. Important helpline numbers are scattered across flyers, WhatsApp groups, and outdated websites.

2. **Civic issues go unreported and unresolved** — Potholes, water leaks, and electricity outages persist because there's no easy way for residents to report them with evidence (photos, location) and track whether anything is being done. People give up reporting because nothing seems to change.

3. **No central place for mutual aid** — A mother in Eden Park has school uniforms her children outgrew. A pensioner in Thinasonke needs food for three days. A student can tutor Grade 10 maths. Today these connections happen by word of mouth, if they happen at all. Plenty of goodwill is wasted simply because people don't know who needs what.

4. **Untrustworthy local services marketplace** — Finding a reliable plumber, electrician, or tutor in the township means relying on a friend's recommendation or gambling on a flyer stapled to a pole. There's no way to see ratings, compare prices, or know if a provider is verified.

5. **Existing solutions are not built for township realities** — Mainstream apps assume data-rich connections, English fluency, and formal business listings. They don't speak to the Ubuntu culture of shared community responsibility.

## Who This App Is For

| User | How They Use It |
|---|---|
| **Thokoza residents** (all ages) | Find emergency numbers and clinic hours in seconds |
| **Parents and caregivers** | Report municipal issues affecting their homes and streets |
| **Community-minded individuals** | Post items they can share or offer skills (tutoring, baking, transport) |
| **People in need** | Request food parcels, school uniforms, transport, or other help discreetly and with dignity |
| **Local tradespeople** (plumbers, electricians, gardeners, hairdressers, tutors) | List their services with ratings and pricing so neighbours can find them |
| **Community leaders and ward councillors** | Monitor reported issues and track which problems are being addressed |

The app is intentionally **lightweight and mobile-first** because the vast majority of township residents access the internet through smartphones with limited data. Big buttons, clear icons, and a simple pink-and-white design make it usable by everyone — from tech-savvy youth to elderly gogos.

## Key Features

### 1. Find Help Near Me

A searchable directory of essential services with one-tap calling and directions:

- **Thokoza Community Clinic** — phone number, operating hours, directions
- **SAPS Police Station** — 24-hour contact and address
- **Water & Electricity Municipal Helpline** — +2711-999-2100, 24/7
- **Emergency Services** — 10111, one-tap call with a prominent emergency banner

Each card shows the service name, phone number, hours of operation, and physical address with a "Directions" button that opens Google Maps. A search bar lets users filter by name, category, or phone number.

### 2. Report Issue

A civic reporting tool that lets residents document problems in their neighbourhood:

- **Issue type dropdown**: Pothole, Water Leak, No Electricity, Street Light Out, Waste/Rubbish
- **Photo upload**: Take a photo or choose from gallery to provide visual evidence
- **Location input**: Specify the street or area where the issue is
- **Description**: Add details about the problem
- **Status tracking**: Every report shows a status badge — Reported, In Progress, or Resolved — that can be updated as the issue progresses

All submitted reports appear in a list below the form, creating a visible record of community issues that anyone can see. This transparency builds accountability.

### 3. Ubuntu Share Board

A community feed inspired by the Ubuntu philosophy — "I am because we are":

- **Two tabs**: Offering Help and Needing Help
- **Post cards** show: the person's name, what they're offering or needing (e.g. "2x school uniform size 12" or "Need food parcel for 3 days"), their location, and a **WhatsApp contact button** that opens a chat directly
- **Post creation form**: Anyone can post what they have to share or what they need, with their name, item description, area, and WhatsApp number
- **Locations covered**: Thokoza, Eden Park, Thinasonke, Germiston, Katlehong, Vosloorus

This is not a marketplace. It's a mutual aid board where neighbours help neighbours — no money changes hands, just goodwill.

### 4. Trusted Services

A directory of vetted local service providers:

- **Categories**: Plumber, Electrician, Tutor, Hairdresser, Gardener
- **Each provider card** shows: name, category icon, star rating (1–5), price range (e.g. R150/call), and a verified badge for checked providers
- **Contact buttons**: Call and WhatsApp on every card
- **Search and filter**: Find providers by name or category
- **Verified badge**: Providers that have been community-verified display a badge so users know who has been vetted

### Design and Accessibility

- **Mobile-first**: Built for smartphones with large touch targets and bottom navigation
- **Light pink and white theme**: Warm, welcoming, and community-focused
- **Bottom navigation** with four clear icons: Find Help, Report, Ubuntu, Services
- **Smooth animations**: Cards slide in, buttons scale on tap, transitions feel natural
- **AI disclaimer**: A footer reminds users to verify providers before making payments

## How It Helps People and Generates Value

### For Residents
- **Saves time in emergencies**: One tap to call 10111 or the municipal helpline instead of searching through contacts
- **Gives a voice**: Residents can document and report issues with photos, creating a public record that's harder to ignore
- **Reduces isolation**: Elderly or vulnerable residents can request help and connect with neighbours through WhatsApp
- **Saves money**: Sharing school uniforms, food, and skills means families spend less

### For Service Providers
- **Free visibility**: A plumber in Thokoza can list their services and get found by neighbours without paying for advertising
- **Builds reputation**: Star ratings and verified badges help good providers stand out
- **Direct contact**: Call and WhatsApp buttons remove friction between provider and customer

### For the Community
- **Strengthens social bonds**: The Ubuntu Share Board turns goodwill into action — people who want to help can find people who need help
- **Creates accountability**: Reported issues with photos and statuses make municipal problems visible and trackable
- **Keeps money in the community**: When residents hire a local tutor or electrician through the app, that money stays in Thokoza

### For Local Government
- **Crowdsourced issue reporting**: Officials can see real-time reports of potholes, leaks, and outages with photos and locations
- **Data on community needs**: The app creates a record of what issues are reported most and where, helping prioritise resources

## Why It's Better Than Existing Solutions

| Challenge | Existing Options | Sawubona Community |
|---|---|---|
| **Finding emergency numbers** | Scattered across paper flyers, outdated websites, or saved in someone's phone | All essential numbers in one searchable place with one-tap calling and directions |
| **Reporting municipal issues** | Municipal call centres with long wait times, no feedback, no photo evidence | Submit with photo and location in under a minute, track status publicly |
| **Mutual aid / sharing** | Word of mouth, scattered WhatsApp groups, or nothing at all | Dedicated board with categories, locations, and WhatsApp integration |
| **Finding local tradespeople** | Flyers on poles, unverified Facebook posts, or relying on a single recommendation | Rated directory with verified badges, price ranges, and direct contact |
| **Accessibility** | Mainstream apps that are data-heavy, English-only, and built for formal businesses | Lightweight, mobile-first, big buttons, designed for township realities |
| **Community focus** | Generic platforms that serve everyone and no one specifically | Built for Thokoza with local areas, local services, and Ubuntu values baked in |

The key difference is **context**. This app was not built for "everyone" — it was built for Thokoza. The locations, the service listings, the Ubuntu philosophy, and the pink-and-white warmth are all intentional choices that make it feel like it belongs to this community, not something imposed on it.

## Tech Stack

- **Frontend**: React + TypeScript + Vite
- **Styling**: Tailwind CSS with custom pink theme
- **Icons**: Lucide React
- **Backend**: Supabase (PostgreSQL database with Row Level Security)
- **Fonts**: Poppins

### Database Tables

| Table | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `reported_issues` | Stores citizen-reported municipal issues with photo, location, and status |
| `ubuntu_posts` | Stores community share board posts (offering or needing help) |
| `trusted_services` | Stores local service provider listings with ratings and verification |

All tables use Row Level Security with public read/write access (single-tenant, no-auth community app).
